Scope and evaluation method

#Permalink to Scope and evaluation method

The evidence collection covers active, review-relevant Drupal Core Accessibility issues: Active, Needs review, Needs work, RTBC and Patch. Fixed, closed and postponed issues are excluded. The snapshot contains 432 active findings, of which 196 carry at least one explicit WCAG issue tag.

38 findings have full comments and merge-request evidence; 394 are metadata-only inventory records. The inventory records identify active issues but do not include comments, merge requests or commit analysis. All findings are unreviewed.

  • Discovery pages the complete Accessibility queue; it does not stop at the first API page.
  • WCAG tags are candidate relevance signals from the issue queue, not conformance determinations.
  • No issue was reproduced against an installed Drupal target in this pass.
  • Open issue reports are treated as draft barrier evidence; they are not a release reproduction.
  • No keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, reduced-motion or forced-colors testing of the product is claimed.
  • No AI was used to assign applicability or an ACR result.
  • Automated coverage is partial: axe/ACT rule mappings show where some evaluation is possible, but no pass/fail result is recorded here. Guidepup can add behavioral screen-reader evidence when a run is recorded; no Guidepup run is claimed in this snapshot.

How the draft results are assigned

#Permalink to How the draft results are assigned

The declared Drupal surface assessment is the baseline. Web and Authoring Tool are assessed surfaces. The active accessibility issue queue is the known-barrier register for those surfaces.

No conformance term is derived from issue presence or absence. Reported issues are listed separately as potential barriers. Does Not Support is not recorded because there is no explicit broad-failure evidence. Not Applicable is used only for documented feature absence. Every other criterion remains Not Evaluated until a reviewer records reviewed evidence.

This is an issue-queue attestation, not a WCAG-EM audit. A reported issue does not prove a barrier in this release, and an empty queue does not prove support. Automated coverage is partial and is reported separately.

The media criteria 1.2.1, 1.2.3, 1.2.4, 1.2.5 and 1.4.2 are Not Applicable because Drupal Core does not provide the relevant audio/video player, live-media feature or automatic audio control. They are not Not Applicable because the queue is empty.

Why this ACR is different

#Permalink to Why this ACR is different

A VPAT is a template; a completed report is an ACR. Most vendor ACRs are periodic, sample-based snapshots reviewed by legal or product management, with the vendor's issue queue kept separate. This report is an issue-queue attestation: it is generated from the full active Drupal Core Accessibility queue and keeps every cited issue linked.

Conventional vendor ACR and this issue-queue attestation
DimensionConventional vendor ACRThis ACR
CadenceAnnual, biennial or procurement-drivenEvery weekly tracker snapshot
EvidenceSelected pages, flows or a WCAG-EM sampleFull active Accessibility queue; comments/MRs where collected
TraceabilitySummary claims, often without issue linksEvery cited issue links to the public Drupal.org source
AuthoringOften folded into web or omittedExplicit Authoring Tool component and separate remarks
ChangeStatic until the next reportIncremental; new issues and status changes appear next snapshot
IndependenceOften a commissioned assessmentCommunity-reported issue evidence; no independent WCAG-EM audit

It is stronger on completeness of known reports, traceability, recency and connection to the remediation backlog. It is weaker on independent page sampling and manual assistive-technology coverage. It is not a WCAG-EM evaluation, a certification or a substitute for a procurement-specific ACR. A missing tagged issue is not evidence of support.

Read the queue as reported barrier evidence. The criterion result is a draft issue-queue attestation subject to human confirmation. See the GSA OpenACR discussion and Drupal's ACR process for the wider model.

Why these accessibility issues matter

#Permalink to Why these accessibility issues matter

Accessibility barriers can prevent people from finding information, completing tasks, communicating and using digital services independently. The barrier arises from the interaction between a person's needs and inaccessible technology, content or environments; disability itself is not the problem.

Accessibility is a global concern

#Permalink to Accessibility is a global concern

The World Health Organization estimates that approximately 1.3 billion people, or 16% of the world's population, experience significant disability. WHO also reports at least 2.2 billion people with near or distance vision impairment, and approximately 430 million people who require rehabilitation for disabling hearing loss.

These estimates use different definitions and overlap. They are context for the scale of accessibility needs, not counts of Drupal users and not estimates of people excluded by this release.

Why remediation matters for Drupal

#Permalink to Why remediation matters for Drupal

Drupal Core is shared software used by many sites and downstream products. A defect in a reusable component can affect multiple deployments, and a fix can benefit them together. But an issue in Core does not prove that every Drupal site contains the barrier: the component may be disabled, the site may use another theme or implementation, the issue may affect only some interactions or roles, and the reported issue may not reproduce in this release. No Drupal installation count is used to estimate affected people.
